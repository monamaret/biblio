A printed page asks to be read at its own pace. The measure, the leading and the width of the margin all set a tempo before a single word is understood.[^1]

The scholarly books of the early seventies knew this. They gave the text room, and they trusted the reader to stay.

[^1]: The same is true on a screen, though screens rarely admit it.
